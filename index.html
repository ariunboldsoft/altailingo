Unit 1: What is Tourism?

Tourism is the temporary short-term movement of people to destinations outside their normal home and workplace.

Job TitleCore Responsibility
Tour GuideEscorting travelers and explaining local history.
Hotel ReceptionistChecking guests in and managing room reservations.

Grammar Tip

Use can + infinitive (e.g., *I can speak English*) to express professional abilities.

Unit 2: World Destinations

Exploring famous international tourist destinations, geographical features, and local customs.

FeatureDescription
Coastal ResortA place by the sea providing lodging and entertainment.
LandmarkAn object or feature of a landscape or town that is easily seen and recognized.

Vocabulary Note

Learn adjectives to describe locations accurately, such as *picturesque*, *bustling*, and *remote*.

Unit 3: Tour Operators

Understanding how tour operators combine travel items into package holidays for customers.

TermDefinition
Package HolidayA holiday that includes transport, accommodation, and tours arranged by an agency.
ItineraryA planned route or journey schedule.

Key Takeaway

Tour operators manage large blocks of tickets and hotel rooms to lower costs for travelers.

Unit 4: Tourist Motivations

Examining why people travel, shifting trends, and the differences between traditional and modern tourism.

MotivationDescription
Leisure & RecreationTraveling to relax, unwind, and escape everyday stress.
Cultural DiscoverySeeking out heritage, authentic food, and local traditions.

Language Spot

Use comparative adjectives when discussing changing trends in visitor choices (e.g., *more popular*, *less crowded*).

Unit 5: Travel Agencies

Focusing on retail travel staff, identifying client needs, and matching customers with ideal holiday products.

SkillAction
ProbingAsking open-ended questions to discover a client's exact holiday budget and preferences.
RecommendingSuggesting specific destinations and handling reservations.

Sales Tip

Always listen carefully to customer requirements before presenting a product proposal.

Unit 6: Transport in Tourism

Covering global transit networks, aviation, rail systems, cruise ships, and timetable management.

Transport TypeKey Term
AviationCheck-in desk, boarding pass, luggage allowance.
MaritimeCabin, deck, cruise itinerary.

Grammar Tip

Use the present simple tense when talking about fixed transport timetables and schedules (e.g., *The train departs at 08:30*).

Unit 7: Accommodation

Hotel facilities, room categories, staffing structures, and managing telephone bookings.

Hotel DepartmentStaff Role
Front OfficeReceptionist, concierge, reservation clerk.
HousekeepingRoom attendant, laundry supervisor.

Key Takeaway

Polite phone etiquette and clear articulation are vital when recording telephone bookings.

Unit 8: Marketing and Promotion

Learning how tourism destinations and products are marketed using promotional campaigns and advertising strategies.

Marketing ToolPurpose
BrochurePrinted or digital booklet detailing holiday packages and prices.
SWOT AnalysisEvaluating Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats.

Vocabulary Reminder

Master superlative adjectives for advertising language (e.g., *the most exciting destination*, *the best value*).

Unit 9: The Airline Industry

Investigating commercial aviation, airport operations, customer care, and environmental considerations.

ConceptDefinition
Carbon OffsetA mechanism enabling travelers to compensate for greenhouse gas emissions.
OverbookingSelling more tickets than available seats to offset passenger no-shows.

Language Spot

Use polite indirect questions when dealing with flight status inquiries (e.g., *Could you tell me when flight BA123 boards?*).

Unit 10: Holidays with a Difference

Exploring alternative tourism sectors, such as adventure travel, ecotourism, and cultural expeditions to remote regions.

Niche TypeFocus Area
EcotourismResponsible travel to natural areas conserving the environment and improving local well-being.
Adventure TravelExploration involving risk, physical exertion, and outdoor skill.

Key Takeaway

Cultural sensitivity and environmental respect are core requirements for specialized niche tourism.

Unit 11: Reservations and Sales

Managing computerized booking systems, understanding booking conditions, and processing payments.

System/TermFunction
GDS (Global Distribution System)Computerized network used for booking airline seats, hotel rooms, and car rentals.
Cancellation PolicyRules regarding refunds when a customer cancels a booking.

Customer Care Tip

Always repeat payment confirmation numbers back to the client clearly to prevent errors.

Unit 12: Airport Departures

Managing the final departure process, terminal services, customs, security clearances, and boarding procedures.

Terminal ZoneActivity
Security CheckpointScanning carry-on luggage and screening passengers.
Boarding GateFinal document check before entering the aircraft.

Language Spot

Use clear imperative instructions for passenger safety control (e.g., *Please place electronic devices in the tray*).
